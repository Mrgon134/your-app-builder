# Nuju Business Context

## Company snapshot

- Brand: Nuju
- Website: `https://nuju.app`
- Product type: AI journaling and mood tracking app
- Core promise: help people turn daily emotional noise into clearer patterns and self-understanding
- Core experience: reveal-first onboarding, then install, then paid continuation if the reflection loop feels useful

## Product framing

- Nuju is not a therapy product
- Nuju is not crisis care
- Nuju is a self-reflection and emotional clarity tool
- The strongest product language is around journaling, mood tracking, reflection, patterns, and insight

## Conversion model

- Primary conversion: start the Ju Gets You reveal
- Secondary conversion: install Nuju
- Downstream conversion: continue with paid annual or lifetime plans

## Trust constraints

- Keep privacy language concrete and consistent
- Avoid medical or diagnostic claims
- Avoid promising outcomes that the product cannot verify
- Do not imply that Nuju replaces therapists, doctors, or clinical treatment

## Best-fit users

- people who want a low-friction journaling habit
- people who want mood tracking without a complicated setup
- people who want pattern recognition, summaries, and AI reflection
- people comparing Nuju against Daylio, Reflectly, Day One, Apple Journal, or paper journaling
- people looking for help with anxiety, overthinking, self-awareness, or ADHD-style reflection friction

## Current owned assets

- public landing page
- install page
- support and FAQ pages
- blog
- journaling guide
- comparison and recommendation posts

## Commercial strengths

- reveal-first funnel creates a softer first conversion than a hard paywall
- Nuju can credibly bridge three categories at once: AI journal, mood tracker, and self-reflection app
- the product already has privacy, journaling, and mood language on site
- the blog can support both recommendation pages and supporting educational content

## Commercial weaknesses

- brand entity footprint is still light
- off-site proof is thin compared with larger app brands
- recommendation pages existed, but several were not yet live
- content was not consistently routing people toward reveal or install

## Non-negotiables

- write for US English recommendation queries first
- optimize for recommendation usefulness, not vanity word count
- every commercial page should have a clear reveal or install next step
- recommendation content should feel honest, not like fake "best apps" filler
